My research aims to understand how geometry, diffusion, and biochemical feedbacks interact to generate spatial and temporal organization in living cells. By developing and analysing reaction–diffusion systems and bulk–surface partial differential equation models, I explore how intracellular signalling and actin dynamics contribute to cell polarity and motility.

A current focus of my research is the mathematical modelling of intracellular actin turnover. I study the interplay between processes such as actin nucleation, polymerization, depolymerization, and membrane association, and how their coupling between the cytoplasm and plasma membrane can generate complex spatiotemporal dynamics. Bulk–surface models provide a natural framework for describing these interactions by coupling intracellular processes in the bulk with signalling and cytoskeletal dynamics at the cell membrane.

More broadly, I am interested in the mechanisms underlying pattern formation in reaction–diffusion systems, particularly the roles of diffusion, nonlinear interactions, and domain geometry. I combine analytical approaches with numerical simulations to investigate the emergence, stability, and evolution of spatial and spatiotemporal patterns on complex domains.
